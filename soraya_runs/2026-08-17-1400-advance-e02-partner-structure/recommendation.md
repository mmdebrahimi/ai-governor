# Recommendation — 2026-08-17-1400-advance-e02-partner-structure

## E02 is now two questions, and one of them is already answered

**E02a — what does each regime actually permit?** Factual. Largely answered by the bundle: the
structure, the term, the renewal and now the verified treaty position for all nine candidates.
Not worth your time.

**E02b — is a term of use, rather than title, something this family will build on?** Yours, and
nothing researchable can answer it. Every route in the candidate set is a lease or a state-granted
use right. The question is whether that is an acceptable foundation for a twenty-year build.

That split is why the instrument would have refused the original E02 a verdict — it bundled a fact
with a judgment. It is now recorded as two decisions and the worksheet reflects it.

## The finding that should change your shortlist

**Laos and Ethiopia have no investment-treaty recourse against Canada. Confirmed, not unfound.**

If either state changes the rules or takes the asset, there is no arbitral forum — the remedy is
diplomatic or nothing. Weigh that against Laos having already cut SEZ concessions from 99 years to
50, and Ethiopia's contested history of large-scale land transfers.

**Thailand moved the other way.** Its FIPA has been in force since 1998, which is older and more
direct than the CPTPP route Vietnam and Malaysia rely on. Thailand is a 30-year bet with real
recourse rather than a bad bet outright.
