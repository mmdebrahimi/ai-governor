# Comparative government policy library — 40 largest economies + an instructive tail

## Status and scope

> **TIER: DISCOVERY. NOT CITED. NOT AN INPUT TO ANY INSTRUMENT.**
>
> Compiled 2026-08-12 from model knowledge, **not** from named primary sources with dates. Nothing
> here has been through `/research-verify`, and it must not be promoted into
> `research_outputs/` (CITED tier) or quoted as established fact without verification. Treat every
> bullet as *"this is reportedly the case, go check it"*.

> **QUARANTINE — read before using this anywhere near `aigov`.**
>
> This file is a catalogue of **what other governments do**. That is precisely the input
> `aigov/decisions.py` and `docs/decision-inventory-protocol.md` exist to keep OUT of the
> derivation: adopting the outward FORM of something that works elsewhere while the function never
> arrives is the documented standard failure of institutional reform, and it is the failure an LLM
> is best at producing.
>
> So: **this file never feeds `build_inventory`, never suggests a capability, never proposes a
> structure, and never answers an elicited field.** It is background reading for a human deciding
> what to *try*, downstream of a derivation that was done from their own decisions. If a policy
> here ever looks like an answer to "what should we have?", the anti-mimicry rail has failed.

## How to read this

- **Ranking basis:** nominal GDP, approximately the 2024–25 ordering. The boundary around 35–40 is
  soft — Nigeria, Chile, Colombia, Romania, Czechia, Portugal, Finland, Pakistan, Iran, New
  Zealand and Kazakhstan all sit near it and swap places depending on the year and source.
- **A GDP ranking is the wrong ranking for this question.** Policy quality is uncorrelated with
  economy size, and the most transferable institutional design tends to come from small states
  that had to be deliberate. Hence the appendix, which is arguably the more useful half.
- **`(contested)`** marks a policy where the evidence is genuinely disputed or the costs are
  serious — included because knowing the argument matters, not as a recommendation.
- Selection favours policies that are **distinctive** (not everyone does it), **legible** (you can
  see the mechanism), and **plausibly portable**. Bad policies are omitted rather than argued with,
  except where a country's notable feature IS the cautionary case.

## 1. United States

- **Federalism as a policy laboratory** — 50 jurisdictions running parallel variants, so reforms
  get tested at state scale before national adoption. Brandeis' "laboratories of democracy".
- **DARPA model** — small agency, no permanent labs, empowered program managers on fixed terms
  with authority to start and kill projects fast. Widely copied (ARPA-E, ARIA, DARPA-style units).
- **Freedom of Information Act (1966)** — statutory presumption of disclosure with enumerated
  exemptions, plus a judicial appeal route. The template most FOI laws descend from.
- **Chapter 11 reorganisation** — bankruptcy that preserves going-concern value and lets a firm
  keep trading under protection, rather than forcing liquidation. A major reason US business
  failure is recoverable.
- **Earned Income Tax Credit** — wage subsidy delivered through the tax system, phased in with
  earnings so it rewards work rather than withdrawing at the first dollar earned.

## 2. China

- **Special Economic Zones** — geographically bounded policy pilots (Shenzhen the archetype) run
  for years before national rollout, so a failed experiment is contained.
- **Sequenced pilot-to-scale** — the general pattern: 试点 (trial point) → provincial extension →
  national law. Policy is treated as something to be tested rather than promulgated.
- **Long-horizon infrastructure planning** — five-year plans with rolling revision, enabling
  capital projects whose payback exceeds any electoral cycle.
- **Cadre performance contracts** — local officials evaluated and promoted on measurable targets.
  *(contested: strong Goodhart effects — GDP and later environmental targets were gamed, data
  falsified, and the metric drifted from the goal.)*

## 3. Germany

- **Dual vocational training (Ausbildung)** — apprenticeship splitting time between employer and
  vocational school, employer-funded, ending in a nationally standardised and portable
  qualification. The main structural answer to youth unemployment.
- **Kurzarbeit (short-time work)** — the state tops up wages for reduced hours so firms cut hours
  instead of headcount in a downturn. Preserved firm-specific human capital through 2009 and 2020;
  widely copied afterwards.
- **Co-determination (Mitbestimmung)** — worker representation on supervisory boards and works
  councils, giving labour a formal voice in restructuring rather than only a strike.
- **Sparkassen / regional public banks** — locally-anchored lenders with a mandate to serve their
  region, sustaining relationship lending to the Mittelstand across cycles.
- **Debt brake (Schuldenbremse)** — constitutional structural-deficit limit. *(contested: credited
  with fiscal credibility, blamed for a decade of infrastructure and defence underinvestment.)*

## 4. Japan

- **Nationally negotiated fee schedule** — a single price list for medical procedures and drugs,
  revised on a fixed cycle. Universal coverage at notably low cost; price control without
  restricting supply choice.
- **Long-term care insurance (2000)** — a separate mandatory social insurance for ageing care,
  decoupled from health insurance, with assessed need levels.
- **Disaster preparedness as standing practice** — strict seismic building codes with retrofit
  programmes, nationwide early warning, and routine population-level drills.
- **Kaizen / continuous improvement in administration** — incremental, front-line-originated
  process improvement institutionalised rather than left to reorganisations.
- **Dedicated high-speed rail corridors** — grade-separated, no mixed traffic, centralised
  control; the safety and punctuality record follows from the segregation.

## 5. India

- **Digital public infrastructure (India Stack)** — identity, payments and consent built as open,
  interoperable public rails that private firms build on rather than as a state monopoly app.
- **UPI** — real-time interoperable retail payments; the strongest existing case that payment
  rails are public infrastructure rather than a private network to be rented.
- **Direct Benefit Transfer** — subsidies paid into bank accounts rather than in kind, cutting the
  intermediary leakage that plagued physical distribution.
- **MGNREGA rural employment guarantee** — a legal right to a fixed number of days of manual work
  at a low statutory wage. Self-targeting: only those who need it turn up.
- **Right to Information Act (2005)** — request rights extending down to local government, with
  penalties on officials for non-response.

## 6. United Kingdom

- **NICE** — an explicit cost-effectiveness threshold body deciding what the health service will
  fund. Makes rationing transparent and arguable instead of implicit and arbitrary.
- **Behavioural Insights Team** — a standing unit running randomised trials on the delivery of
  government services. Institutionalised the "test the letter before you send 10 million of them".
- **Operational central bank independence (1997)** — rate-setting delegated to a committee against
  a published inflation target, with mandated public explanation when missed.
- **Office for Budget Responsibility** — independent forecasting so the government cannot mark its
  own fiscal homework.
- **Green Belt and use-class planning** — *(contested: effective at containing sprawl, and a major
  identified cause of the housing shortage and its cost.)*

## 7. France

- **Standardised nuclear fleet** — a small number of reactor designs built repeatedly, so learning
  and regulatory approval amortise across units. The clearest example of decarbonised baseload via
  standardisation rather than novelty.
- **Family policy as infrastructure** — subsidised crèche places, universal allowances and school
  from age three, sustaining relatively high fertility and maternal employment together.
- **Career civil service with formal training** — a professional administrative corps trained for
  the role. *(contested: same institution is charged with producing a closed, self-reproducing
  elite.)*
- **Right to disconnect (2017)** — a statutory obligation on larger firms to negotiate limits on
  out-of-hours contact.

## 8. Italy

- **Cassa integrazione guadagni** — a long-standing wage supplementation fund that keeps workers
  attached to the firm during suspensions. The ancestor of the modern short-time-work idea.
- **Industrial districts** — dense geographic clusters of specialised SMEs sharing suppliers,
  skills and tacit knowledge. Studied as a model of competitiveness without scale.
- **Heritage protection apparatus** — statutory superintendencies with veto power over alterations
  to protected fabric. *(contested: preserves an irreplaceable stock, also a byword for delay.)*

## 9. Canada

- **Points-based immigration (Express Entry)** — published, weighted, transparent selection
  criteria with a ranked pool. Applicants can compute their own odds.
- **Canada Child Benefit** — a single income-tested, non-taxable monthly payment replacing a
  patchwork of credits; produced a large measured fall in child poverty.
- **Prudential banking regulation** — conservative capital and mortgage rules under a single
  supervisor; no bank failures through 2008.
- **Equalisation transfers** — constitutionalised fiscal transfers so poorer provinces can deliver
  comparable services at comparable tax rates.

## 10. Brazil

- **Bolsa Família** — conditional cash transfer paid to the mother, conditioned on school
  attendance and vaccination. The most-copied social programme design of the last 25 years.
- **Pix** — central-bank-built instant payment system, free for individuals, adopted at
  extraordinary speed; a state-as-platform counterpoint to card networks.
- **SUS** — constitutionally guaranteed universal health system in a middle-income country.
- **Flex-fuel mandate and ethanol infrastructure** — vehicle and distribution standards that made
  a biofuel actually substitutable at the pump rather than a niche.
- **Electronic voting** — nationwide same-day electronic count. *(contested: auditability of
  paperless voting is a live and unresolved criticism.)*

## 11. Russia

- **Gosuslugi services portal** — consolidated digital access to state services, genuinely
  reducing counter visits.
- **Flat personal income tax (introduced 2001)** — radical simplification credited with improving
  compliance in a low-capacity collection environment. *(Later partially unwound.)*

> Honest note: the transferable-policy yield here is thin, and the binding constraints on Russian
> governance are rule-of-law and corruption issues that no individual policy in this list offsets.

## 12. South Korea

- **KONEPS e-procurement** — near-universal electronic public purchasing on one platform;
  transparency as an anti-corruption mechanism rather than an audit function.
- **Pandemic legal preparedness** — statutory testing, tracing and data powers legislated after
  the 2015 MERS failure, which is why the 2020 response was fast.
- **R&D intensity by deliberate policy** — among the highest R&D-to-GDP ratios globally, sustained
  by tax credits and public institutes.
- **Cultural export promotion** — state support for content industries as an export sector, with
  financing and market access rather than content control.
- **National broadband buildout** — early, subsidised, near-universal high-speed access treated as
  infrastructure.

## 13. Australia

- **HECS/HELP income-contingent student loans** — tuition repaid as a share of income above a
  threshold, collected through the tax system, written off if income never arrives. Invented here
  and copied widely; removes the risk that deters poorer applicants.
- **Compulsory superannuation** — mandatory employer contributions to a portable retirement
  account, producing one of the world's largest pension pools.
- **1996 firearms buyback and uniform licensing** — a rapid, compensated, national response after
  a mass shooting.
- **Plain tobacco packaging** — world-first; survived trade and investor challenge, then copied.
- **Productivity Commission** — an independent body that publicly reviews policy on request, with
  published reasoning.

## 14. Mexico

- **Progresa / Oportunidades** — the original conditional cash transfer, and crucially the first
  designed with a randomised evaluation built in from the start. It is why CCTs spread: there was
  evidence.
- **CONEVAL** — an autonomous body that measures poverty and evaluates social programmes
  independently of the ministries that run them.

## 15. Spain

- **Organ donation system** — presumed consent combined with a dedicated transplant coordinator
  inside each hospital and a national coordinating organisation. Sustained world-leading donation
  rates; the coordinator role, not the opt-out default, is the part that actually transfers.
- **ERTE furlough scheme** — short-time work adapted at speed in 2020.
- **High-speed rail network** — the largest in Europe, built on standardised procurement.
- **Renewables buildout** — auction-based procurement driving solar and wind capacity.

## 16. Indonesia

- **JKN** — a single-payer health scheme covering the majority of a very large, dispersed
  population within about a decade of launch.
- **Dana Desa (village fund)** — a formula-based fiscal transfer direct to village government with
  local decision rights over use.
- **Fuel subsidy reform (2014–15)** — a regressive universal subsidy cut and the fiscal space
  explicitly redirected to health, infrastructure and targeted transfers. The textbook sequencing
  for a politically hard reform.

## 17. Netherlands

- **Water management (Delta Works, Room for the River)** — centuries-old institutional capacity
  with independent, tax-raising water boards; the shift from "build higher walls" to "give the
  river space" is a rare example of an institution reversing its own paradigm.
- **Polder model** — institutionalised tripartite bargaining between government, employers and
  unions, producing negotiated rather than imposed adjustment.
- **Cycling infrastructure and Sustainable Safety doctrine** — separated networks designed so that
  predictable human error does not become a fatality.
- **Funded collective pensions** — occupational schemes with collective risk-sharing, consistently
  top-ranked internationally.
- **Statutory equal treatment of part-time work** — a right to reduced hours without loss of
  pro-rata pay or status; a major driver of labour force participation.

## 18. Turkey

- **DASK compulsory earthquake insurance** — a mandatory pooled catastrophe scheme with state
  reinsurance backing.
- **Health Transformation Programme (2003)** — rapid expansion of coverage and family medicine
  with measurable gains in access and outcomes.

## 19. Saudi Arabia

- **Sovereign-wealth-led diversification** — an explicit strategy of converting a depleting
  resource into a portfolio of non-resource sectors. *(contested and unresolved: execution and
  returns are not yet demonstrated, and the labour-market and social preconditions are the hard
  part.)*
- **Government service digitisation** — mandated online delivery with published ministry
  performance measures.

## 20. Switzerland

- **Direct democracy** — binding facultative referendums and popular initiatives. The systemic
  effect is anticipatory: government drafts legislation it expects to survive a referendum, so
  consultation happens before rather than after.
- **Debt brake (2003)** — a cyclically-adjusted balance rule with a compensation account for
  overruns. Widely regarded as the best-designed fiscal rule in practice.
- **Fiscal federalism with cantonal competition** — meaningful tax and spending autonomy at
  cantonal and communal level, with the accountability that follows from raising your own revenue.
- **Militia system** — a part-time legislature of people with ordinary careers.
  *(contested: strengthens ties to real life, weakens capacity against a professional executive.)*
- **Apprenticeship system** — as Germany, with roughly two-thirds of young people entering
  vocational tracks that lead to real careers rather than being a residual.

## 21. Poland

- **Family 500+ child benefit** — a large, simple, near-universal per-child payment; sharp
  measured reduction in child poverty. *(contested: weak fertility effect for the fiscal cost, and
  a reduction in maternal labour supply.)*
- **Special economic zones** — investment incentives geographically targeted at post-industrial
  regions.

## 22. Taiwan

- **vTaiwan / Join digital deliberation** — structured online consultation using opinion-clustering
  (Polis) to surface where consensus actually exists before regulation is drafted. The most serious
  working attempt at scaling deliberation rather than polling.
- **Single-payer NHI with a smart card** — one card carrying entitlement and record, giving both
  low administrative cost and real-time system visibility.
- **2020 pandemic response** — integration of health insurance and border data, plus a
  transparent, rationed mask distribution with a public real-time stock map.
- **ITRI industrial policy** — a public research institute that deliberately spins out and
  privatises firms rather than retaining them; TSMC is the canonical output.

## 23. Belgium

- **Automatic wage indexation** — wages tracked to a price index by law, preserving real income in
  inflation. *(contested: the standard critique is a wage-price loop and competitiveness loss.)*
- **Compulsory voting** — turnout consistently above 85%, so the electorate is not a self-selected
  subset.

## 24. Argentina

- **Asignación Universal por Hijo** — a near-universal child allowance for informal-sector and
  unemployed households, reaching people the contributory system structurally missed.

> Honest note: Argentina is more instructive as the cautionary case on monetary and fiscal
> institutions than as a source of policies to copy.

## 25. Sweden

- **Principle of public access (1766)** — the oldest freedom-of-information regime, with a default
  that official documents are public and a registry making them findable. Old enough to be
  cultural rather than merely legal.
- **Fiscal framework after the 1990s crisis** — a surplus target, multi-year expenditure ceilings
  and an independent fiscal council; a country that legislated its way out of a fiscal crisis and
  then stayed out.
- **Job security councils (omställningsfonder)** — employer-funded, jointly-governed bodies that
  handle retraining and placement for the redundant, separate from the state employment service.
- **Reserved parental leave months** — non-transferable months for each parent, so leave is lost
  unless the second parent takes it. A design that changed behaviour where exhortation had not.
- **School vouchers with for-profit providers** — *(contested: the clearest natural experiment in
  marketised schooling, and the evidence on segregation and grade inflation is not favourable.)*

## 26. Ireland

- **Citizens' Assemblies** — randomly selected citizens deliberating over months with expert
  evidence, producing recommendations that then went to referendum. It broke two decades of
  deadlock on genuinely divisive constitutional questions, and is the strongest existing evidence
  that sortition scales to hard problems.
- **IDA Ireland** — a single, well-resourced agency owning foreign investment attraction end to
  end, including aftercare of existing investors.
- **Corporate tax strategy** — *(contested: effective for Ireland, and a live example of a policy
  whose gains are substantially other countries' losses.)*

## 27. Israel

- **Yozma** — a government venture fund-of-funds that took the downside and capped its upside to
  draw in foreign VC, then deliberately privatised itself once the market existed. The rare
  industrial policy with a built-in exit.
- **Innovation Authority matching grants** — R&D support repaid as royalties on success, so the
  state shares upside rather than only funding failure.
- **Conscription as a talent pipeline** — technical units functioning as a de facto training and
  network-formation system feeding the civilian technology sector.

## 28. Norway

- **Government Pension Fund Global** — resource revenue saved into a diversified external fund,
  with a fiscal rule limiting annual spending to an estimate of the real return. Solves the
  resource curse by decoupling spending from the extraction rate.
- **Petroleum tax regime** — a high marginal rate combined with state participation in exploration
  costs, so the state shares risk as well as revenue and investment does not stall.
- **EV transition via tax design** — sustained exemptions on purchase and use taxes rather than
  mandates; produced the world's fastest fleet turnover.
- **Public tax records** — individual income and tax data publicly accessible.
  *(contested: strong transparency and equality norm, real privacy cost.)*
- **Rehabilitative corrections** — prison conditions and reintegration designed around post-release
  outcomes; comparatively low reoffending.

## 29. United Arab Emirates

- **Whole-of-government digital delivery** — service delivery consolidated and measured, with
  published ministerial performance indicators.
- **Long-term residency reform (golden visa)** — decoupling residence from a single employer's
  sponsorship for defined categories, addressing the core weakness of a temporary-migration model.

## 30. Austria

- **Vienna social housing** — municipal and limited-profit housing at a scale (a majority of
  residents) that sets the price in the private market rather than serving as a residual safety
  net. The scale is the mechanism; small-scale copies do not reproduce the effect.
- **Klimaticket** — a single flat-rate annual ticket valid on all public transport nationwide,
  eliminating the fare-complexity barrier to modal shift.
- **Apprenticeship system** — as Germany and Switzerland.

## 31. Singapore

- **HDB housing with ownership** — public housing built for purchase on long leases, with ethnic
  integration quotas per block. Public provision that produces owners rather than tenants.
- **Central Provident Fund** — a single mandatory savings vehicle with earmarked accounts for
  housing, health and retirement, so one contribution stream funds the three big life risks.
- **Electronic road pricing** — gantry-based congestion charging with rates varied by road, time
  and observed speed; pricing tuned to a target level of service rather than a fixed fee.
- **Well-paid, meritocratic civil service** — senior salaries benchmarked to private-sector
  comparators, explicitly as corruption prevention and talent retention.
- **Water self-sufficiency strategy** — reclaimed water, desalination, catchment and imports
  managed as one portfolio against an explicit strategic vulnerability.

## 32. Thailand

- **Universal Coverage Scheme** — universal health coverage achieved at a low income level, with
  an explicit benefit package and a purchaser-provider split. The standing counter-example to
  "wait until you are rich enough".
- **Village and Urban Community Fund** — capital transferred to locally-managed revolving funds.

## 33. Vietnam

- **Đổi Mới sequencing** — agricultural decollectivisation and price liberalisation before
  industrial and financial opening; growth without the output collapse seen in shock-therapy cases.
- **Basic education performance** — learning outcomes far above what the income level predicts,
  attributed to teacher accountability and instructional focus rather than spending.

## 34. Bangladesh

- **Community health worker model at scale** — a large paid and trained cadre delivering basic
  care and referral, achieving child and maternal mortality gains far ahead of income growth.
- **Oral rehydration therapy campaign** — door-to-door teaching of a home-mixable treatment; one
  of the highest-return public health interventions ever run.
- **Female labour force participation via export manufacturing** — garment sector growth as the
  channel for a large social shift, with attendant safety failures that forced the Accord
  inspection regime. *(contested on labour conditions.)*
- **State tolerance of large NGO delivery** — permitting BRAC and others to operate at
  quasi-state scale rather than defending a monopoly on service provision.

## 35. Philippines

- **4Ps conditional cash transfer** — a CCT operating across an archipelago with weak
  administrative reach, with independent impact evaluation.
- **Migrant worker protection apparatus** — pre-departure regulation, contract standards and
  consular support treating labour export as a governed system rather than an outflow.

## 36. Denmark

- **Flexicurity** — the explicit trade: weak employment protection, strong income protection, and
  active retraining. Security is attached to the worker, not the job. Requires all three legs;
  importing only the first is the common failure.
- **District heating** — municipally-planned heat networks with waste heat and combined generation,
  a structural rather than behavioural decarbonisation of heating.
- **Offshore wind and the Ørsted transition** — a state-controlled utility deliberately converted
  from fossil to offshore wind, plus the tender design that drove costs down globally.
- **Mandatory digital post and MitID** — a single digital identity plus a legal digital mailbox
  for official correspondence, so digitisation is the default rather than an option.

## 37. Malaysia

- **Employees Provident Fund** — a long-established mandatory savings scheme with a strong
  contribution record.
- **Islamic finance regulatory framework** — a dual regulatory and standards regime that built a
  genuine international position in a specialised sector.
- **Bumiputera preference policy** — *(contested: credited with defusing ethnic conflict and
  building a middle class, criticised for entrenchment, rent-seeking and emigration of the
  excluded.)*

## 38. Egypt

- **Takaful and Karama** — targeted conditional and unconditional transfers replacing part of a
  costly universal subsidy system, with a beneficiary registry.
- **Subsidy reform via smart cards** — rationing and targeting of bread and fuel subsidies through
  an identity-linked card system.

## 39. Hong Kong

- **ICAC (1974)** — an anti-corruption commission independent of the police, with combined
  investigation, prevention and education arms and its own reporting line. The single most-copied
  anti-corruption institutional design.
- **Rail-plus-property value capture** — the transit operator granted development rights above
  stations, funding rail from the land value the rail itself creates. Profitable urban rail.
- **Simple territorial tax system** — few taxes, low rates, narrow base, minimal compliance cost.
- *(Note: the post-2020 constitutional changes materially altered the governance context in which
  all of the above sat; the institutional designs and their current independence are separate
  questions.)*

## 40. South Africa

- **1996 Constitution and Constitutional Court** — justiciable socio-economic rights with a court
  willing to order remedies, and a widely-studied drafting process.
- **Truth and Reconciliation Commission** — conditional amnesty exchanged for full public
  disclosure; the reference design for transitional justice, and for its limits.
- **Chapter 9 institutions** — constitutionally independent accountability offices (Public
  Protector, Auditor-General) with direct reporting to the legislature.
  *(contested in practice: the design has proven only as strong as appointments to it.)*

# Appendix — outside the GDP-40, and disproportionately instructive

Small states had to be deliberate. This is where the highest density of portable institutional
design sits.

## Estonia

- **X-Road** — a federated data exchange layer with no central database: agencies query each other
  over a standard, logged protocol. The architectural decision that made everything else possible.
- **Once-only principle** — it is unlawful for the state to ask a citizen for data it already
  holds. This is an obligation on government, not a convenience feature.
- **Citizen-visible access log** — every official lookup of your record is logged and inspectable
  by you, with the ability to challenge it. Surveillance is made symmetrical instead of prohibited.
- **Digital identity as mandatory infrastructure** — one legally-binding identity for public and
  private use, which is why adoption is universal rather than partial.
- **e-Residency** — a transferable digital business identity for non-residents; a small state
  exporting its administrative capacity as a service.

## New Zealand

- **Fiscal Responsibility / Public Finance Act** — legislated transparency requirements and
  regular published fiscal strategy, rather than numerical limits. Constrains by disclosure.
- **Wellbeing Budget framework** — budget allocation assessed against a published set of wellbeing
  indicators rather than output volumes alone.
- **MMP electoral reform** — a proportional system adopted by referendum after the public
  concluded the previous one misrepresented them.
- **Treaty settlement process** — a standing negotiated mechanism for historical claims, including
  legal personhood for a river and a national park as a settlement instrument.
- **Predator Free 2050** — a long-horizon ecological goal with a specified end date, funded across
  political cycles.

## Finland

- **Teacher professionalisation** — a research master's requirement, high selectivity, and
  professional autonomy in place of inspection and standardised testing.
- **Housing First** — permanent housing provided unconditionally and before treatment compliance,
  not as a reward for it. The only national programme to have substantially ended long-term
  homelessness.
- **Maternity package** — a standard box of infant supplies given to every expectant parent,
  historically conditioned on attending prenatal care.
- **Comprehensive national security / preparedness model** — cross-sector stockpiling and
  continuity planning as a standing peacetime institution.

## Iceland

- **Equal pay certification** — the burden reversed: employers above a size threshold must obtain
  certification that they pay equally, rather than employees having to prove they do not.
- **Equal, non-transferable parental leave** — reserved months for each parent, driving the
  world's most equal take-up.

## Uruguay

- **Plan Ceibal** — one connected laptop per child, nationwide, with the teaching and content
  programme built alongside rather than after the hardware.
- **Regulated cannabis market** — state-controlled production and pharmacy distribution with
  registered users; regulation designed to displace the illicit market rather than to grow a
  legal one.
- **Near-fully renewable electricity** — a rapid grid transition achieved through auction design
  and grid investment without subsidy on the scale used elsewhere.

## Rwanda

- **Imihigo performance contracts** — public, signed performance commitments by officials with
  published scoring against them.
- **Community-based health insurance (Mutuelles)** — near-universal coverage financed through
  income-banded community premiums.
- **Umuganda** — a scheduled monthly community works obligation.
  *(contested: real infrastructure and cohesion output; also compulsory, in a state with serious
  civil-liberties criticism. The policy and the political context should not be separated.)*
- **Drone medical delivery** — regulatory approval for beyond-line-of-sight autonomous blood and
  vaccine delivery ahead of far richer states. Leapfrogging by permitting rather than by building.

## Georgia

- **Public service halls** — one-stop physical and digital counters consolidating registries and
  permits; transaction counts and times published.
- **Police reform by dismissal and rebuild** — the traffic police were abolished wholesale and
  re-recruited at higher pay rather than reformed incrementally. The strongest case for
  discontinuous rather than gradual anti-corruption reform.
- **Radical registry simplification** — property and business registration collapsed to days,
  removing the discretion that rents were extracted from.

## Costa Rica

- **Abolition of the military (1949)** — the defence budget explicitly and durably redirected to
  education and health. A constitutional commitment that has held for 75 years.
- **Payments for ecosystem services** — landowners paid for forest retention funded by a fuel tax,
  reversing national deforestation.
- **Near-fully renewable grid** — hydro, geothermal and wind portfolio built over decades.

## Botswana

- **Diamond revenue management** — a negotiated joint venture with the mining company plus the
  Pula Fund and a sustainable-budget rule; the standing counter-example to the resource curse in
  Africa.

## Chile

- **Structural balance fiscal rule** — the budget set against trend output and a long-run copper
  price estimated by independent expert panels, so revenue windfalls are saved automatically
  rather than by an act of political will each year. The panel design is the transferable part.
- **Copper stabilisation and pension reserve funds** — the savings vehicles the rule feeds.

## Portugal

- **Drug decriminalisation (2001)** — possession moved out of the criminal system into
  administrative "dissuasion commissions", coupled with a large expansion of treatment. The
  treatment expansion, not the decriminalisation alone, is what the evidence supports.

## What to actually take from this

Six patterns recur across the entries above far more than any specific programme does:

1. **Pilot before national rollout, and bound the pilot geographically.** SEZs, state
   laboratories, 试点 sequencing.
2. **Attach security to the person, not the position.** Flexicurity, income-contingent loans,
   portable pensions, job security councils.
3. **Build rails, not applications.** X-Road, India Stack, UPI, Pix — the state defines a protocol
   and lets others build; it does not ship the app.
4. **Delegate the number to an independent body and publish the reasoning.** NICE, OBR, CONEVAL,
   Chile's copper panels, independent central banks.
5. **Design the default, not the exhortation.** Reserved parental leave months, opt-out donation
   plus coordinators, once-only data, automatic saving.
6. **Where a windfall is temporary, legislate the saving rule before the windfall peaks.**
   Norway, Chile, Botswana — and the absence of it everywhere the resource curse bit.

And the honest caveat that applies to all of it: **each of these is load-bearing on
preconditions that are usually invisible from outside.** Vienna's housing works at 60% market
share and not at 5%. Flexicurity needs all three legs. Spain's donation rate comes from the
hospital coordinator role rather than the opt-out default everyone copies. Singapore's civil
service pay assumes the recruitment and enforcement system around it. The failure mode is
importing the visible instrument and not the precondition — which is the same failure this
repository's anti-mimicry rail exists to prevent, one level up.
